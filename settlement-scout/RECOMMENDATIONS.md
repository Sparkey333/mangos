# Best Settlement-Finder Apps & Services (2026) — and the gaps Settlement Scout fills

Research snapshot as of **June 2026**. Things move fast in this space (new apps
launch monthly); always re-verify before trusting any single tool, and confirm
every claim on the **official settlement website** before filing.

## TL;DR recommendation

No single app does everything well. The strongest setup today is to **run 2–3
finders in parallel** so nothing slips through, then use a planner to actually
file and track deadlines. The most-recommended combination right now:

| Use it for | Pick |
|---|---|
| Best free auto-finder (link bank/cards, passive alerts) | **Catch** |
| Find → file inside the app (incl. mailed claims via "Print Hub") | **Class Action Buddy** |
| Sleek finder + bank-linked alerts | **Settlemate** |
| Largest curated U.S. database / news | **Top Class Actions**, **ClassAction.org** |
| Automatic regulatory refunds (no app needed) | **FTC refund pages** (e.g. Invitation Homes) |

## The landscape

**Auto-finders (link your accounts, get alerts).** *Catch* is widely cited as the
best free option — links cards/bank, surfaces every potential claim, takes no cut.
*Settlemate* and *ClaimHunt* do similar matching against your purchase history.
Trade-off: you hand over bank/transaction access for matching power.

**Find-and-file apps.** *Class Action Buddy* is notable for taking you from
discovery to a filed claim without leaving the app, and handling mail-in
settlements via a Print Hub. *Claimly*, *ZapSettle*, *ClassyAction*, and *Claim*
are newer entrants with quick questionnaire-based matching.

**Databases / media.** *Top Class Actions* connects to one of the largest U.S.
class-action databases (product defects, data breaches, employment, consumer
fraud). *ClassAction.org* and *ClaimDepot* publish open settlements with
eligibility details and citations. *Open Class Actions* curates a newsletter.

**Regulatory redress (not an app).** Agencies like the **FTC** mail refunds
directly — e.g. the Invitation Homes junk-fee redress (~$47.2M to ~444k renters,
**automatic, no claim form**). These never show up in consumer apps because there
is nothing to "file"; you just need a current address.

### Common complaints (from reviews / Reddit-style feedback)
- Many apps only **link out to a PDF/portal** instead of truly filing.
- Bank-linking is a **privacy trade-off** some users dislike.
- Coverage gaps: most apps miss **regulatory refunds**, **mail-in-only** claims,
  niche **tenant/employment** suits, and **state-specific** classes.
- **No deadline tracking / scheduling** — easy to miss a postmark date.
- Nothing reuses the collected data as a **structured dataset** you own.

## Where Settlement Scout is different (the one-stop-shop thesis)

This project is designed to cover the gaps above rather than re-clone a finder:

1. **Transparent, explainable eligibility matching** — every yes/no/maybe is
   backed by a stated reason (good for trust *and* for ML training labels).
2. **Covers all claim types** in one model: class actions, **regulatory/FTC
   auto-refunds**, mail-in claims, watchlists for recurring TCPA/data-breach suits.
3. **Deadline-driven plans + tentative schedules** — works backward from each
   postmark/claim date.
4. **Drafted outreach** (claim-membership inquiry, records request) — review and
   send yourself; **never auto-sent**.
5. **You own the data** — structured JSON/CSV export built for training your other
   Law / AI projects.
6. **Pluggable sources** with **robots.txt-respecting** adapters; ships with
   curated, cited seed data so it works offline and within the rules.
