# Legal Clean-Room — Stay on the *Tunic* Side of the Line

The single rule that makes this whole venture safe and sellable. Read it before you name a
character or import an asset. *(General guidance, not legal advice — for contracts/trademarks,
consult an attorney or a service like Cooley GO / a games lawyer.)*

---

## The core principle

- **Game mechanics & genres = NOT copyrightable.** You may legally make a game that *plays* exactly
  like Metroid, Zelda, or Pokémon. This is the "idea–expression dichotomy."
- **Specific creative expression = fully protected.** Characters, names, logos, art, sprites,
  music, maps, and dialogue are owned. Touch none of Nintendo's.
- **Nintendo enforces harder than anyone.** They've killed AM2R, Pokémon Uranium/Prism, countless
  ROM hacks — and they win. There is **no "modified enough" safe harbor** for using their assets.

> **Verdict:** Build an *original* game in a Nintendo-defined genre (the *Tunic* / *Stardew* /
> *Cassette Beasts* model). Your custom guitar/drums soundtrack and original world are the whole
> point — and they're 100% yours.

---

## ✅ Do / ❌ Don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use genre mechanics (dash, double-jump, ability-gated map) | Use Mario, Link, Samus, Pikachu, or any Nintendo character |
| Create original characters, names, world, story | Reuse Nintendo names, logos, item names, place names |
| Compose your own music (your guitar/drums!) | Sample or "remix" Nintendo music — even covers need licenses |
| Draw/generate your own art in your own style | Trace/import Nintendo sprites, art, or maps |
| Take *inspiration* from how a game feels | Market with "like Zelda" using Nintendo's trademarks in your title/store |
| Pick a title you can trademark-clear | Pick a title confusingly similar to a Nintendo IP |

## Trademark vs. copyright (both matter)

- **Copyright** protects the *art/music/code/text*. Make yours original.
- **Trademark** protects *names/logos/brand*. Before locking your **game title and studio name**,
  search the **USPTO** (<https://tmsearch.uspto.gov>) and just Google + Steam-search it. Avoid
  names that could confuse buyers with an existing brand.

## Fonts, audio & third-party assets

- **Fonts** have licenses — verify "commercial use / embedding" (Google Fonts is safe: <https://fonts.google.com>).
- **Free asset libraries**: confirm the license per asset. **CC0** (Kenney, Poly Haven, ambientCG)
  = use freely, even commercially, no attribution required. **CC-BY** = attribution required.
  Some itch/OpenGameArt assets are non-commercial — check each.
- **Keep a `CREDITS.md`** listing every third-party asset + its license + source URL. Stores and
  buyers expect it; it's also your audit trail.

## AI-generated assets — the fine print

- **Commercial terms vary by tool.** Confirm each service grants commercial rights to *output*
  (most paid tiers do: Firefly, Scenario, Meshy, ElevenLabs paid). Free tiers sometimes don't.
- **Training data matters.** Prefer tools trained on licensed/owned data (Adobe Firefly markets
  this) or **train on your own art** to stay clean and consistent.
- **Copyrightability of pure AI output is unsettled** (the US Copyright Office currently won't
  register works lacking human authorship). Your hand-editing, arrangement, and selection add the
  human authorship — another reason to curate, not dump.
- **Store disclosure:** Steam requires disclosing AI use; check Apple/Google policies too.

## Store content rules (quick pointers)

- **Steam (Steamworks):** $100 Steam Direct fee/app; AI-disclosure questionnaire; standard content
  rules. <https://partner.steamgames.com>
- **Apple App Store:** $99/yr; review guidelines; needs original IP. <https://developer.apple.com>
- **Google Play:** $25 once; policy center. <https://play.google.com/console>
- **Nintendo eShop:** apply to the Nintendo Developer Portal with your *original* game. <https://developer.nintendo.com>

## A 60-second clean-room checklist (run before every milestone)

1. Every shipped asset is **mine, CC0/licensed, or AI with commercial rights** — and logged in `CREDITS.md`.
2. No Nintendo (or other) **names, characters, logos, music, or art** anywhere.
3. **Title + studio name** trademark-searched and clear.
4. **Fonts** cleared for commercial embedding.
5. **AI disclosure** prepared for each store that asks.
6. Music is **my own recording**.
