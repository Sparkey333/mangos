# Feasibility: Building & Selling a Nintendo-Genre-Inspired Indie Game (Solo)

## Context

Goal: take a classic Nintendo game (N64 or earlier), "clone and modify" it with an original
story, voice acting, and a custom **electric-guitar + drums** soundtrack, and sell it on the
App Store, Google Play, Steam, and/or the Nintendo eShop — in the spirit of *Tunic* and
*Pokémon*-likes, but pushing further with original live-instrument music.

This document answers: **which 3 classic-Nintendo genres are the most realistic for a solo
dev to build into a sellable original game, and which storefronts are viable** — and it
corrects one critical misconception about legality up front, because that single fact reshapes
the whole plan.

---

## ⚠️ The legal reality (read this first — it changes the framing)

**You cannot clone and sell an actual Nintendo game.** Nintendo is the most litigious IP
holder in gaming. They have killed AM2R (a Metroid II remake), Pokémon Uranium, Pokémon Prism,
and thousands of ROM hacks and fan games — and they win. Using their **characters (Mario, Link,
Samus, Pikachu), names, logos, art, music, level layouts, or sprites** = copyright + trademark
infringement. There is no "modify it enough" safe harbor for using their assets.

**But here is what IS 100% legal and is a proven business:**

- **Game mechanics and genres are not copyrightable** (the "idea–expression dichotomy"). You
  can legally make a game that plays *exactly* like Zelda, Metroid, or Pokémon as long as every
  piece of *expression* — art, characters, names, story, music, world — is your own.
- This is literally what the reference games did:
  - **Tunic** = *Zelda* mechanics, original fox hero, original world, original music. Sold
    >1M copies, won awards.
  - **Stardew Valley** = *Harvest Moon* mechanics, original everything. One developer (Eric
    Barone). Sold >30M copies.
  - **Cassette Beasts / Coromon / Monster Sanctuary / Nexomon** = *Pokémon* mechanics, original
    monsters. Successful on Steam + mobile + Switch.

**So the real plan is not "clone a Nintendo game." It is "build an original game in a
Nintendo-defined genre."** That is the Tunic path, it is legal, and your custom soundtrack
becomes a genuine selling point rather than a liability. Everything below assumes this framing.

## The Top 3 Genres (ranked for a solo dev who wants music to shine)

Ranking criteria: (1) achievable solo, (2) proven commercial track record on the target stores,
(3) the genre puts **music front-and-center** so a guitar/drums soundtrack is a real
differentiator, (4) legally clean.

### #1 — Metroidvania (inspired by *Metroid*, NES/SNES)
- **Why it wins:** Best music showcase of any genre — atmospheric exploration tracks +
  high-energy boss themes are *made* for shredding electric guitar and drums. The most proven
  indie genre on every storefront, and very solo-friendly (2D tilemap worlds, reusable tilesets,
  a handful of abilities that gate exploration).
- **Proof it sells:** Hollow Knight, Ori and the Blind Forest, Axiom Verge (made by ONE person,
  Tom Happ, who also did much of the audio), Bloodstained, Animal Well (also essentially one
  developer). Axiom Verge is the closest precedent to this exact ambition: solo dev,
  Metroid-style, self-made soundtrack.
- **Scope:** Achievable. Biggest cost is hand-crafted map/art. High music demand (10–20 tracks)
  is a *feature* here, not a bug.

### #2 — Top-down / Light-3D Action-Adventure (inspired by *Zelda*, NES/N64)
- **Why it's here:** The literal *Tunic* lane. Dungeon-puzzle-combat loops, secrets, an iconic
  main theme. Music carries the mood (overworld, dungeon dread, boss intensity) — perfect for a
  signature live-instrument score.
- **Proof it sells:** Tunic, Death's Door, Blossom Tales, Ittle Dew, CrossCode. All successful,
  all on Steam + Switch, most on mobile too.
- **Scope:** Achievable in 2D top-down. **Avoid 3D** (like Ocarina of Time) solo — 3D modeling,
  animation, and camera work balloon scope. Tunic *looks* 3D but is a tiny team using heavy
  asset reuse and a clever isometric trick.

### #3 — Creature-Collector RPG (inspired by *Pokémon*) — *favorite, with a caveat*
- **Why it's here:** Huge market; turn-based battle themes + route exploration music fit the
  soundtrack well. Strong commercial proof on mobile AND Steam AND Switch.
- **Proof it sells:** Cassette Beasts, Coromon, Monster Sanctuary, Nexomon, Temtem.
- **⚠️ The caveat — this is the hardest of the three solo.** Creature-collectors are *content
  factories*: 50–150 original monsters (each needs design, sprites, multiple animations, stats,
  movesets, type balancing), a sprawling world, an item/economy system, and a battle engine.
  That's the most art + design + balancing work of any genre here. Doable, but budget 2–3× the
  Metroidvania timeline. For the *Pokémon* feel with less scope, a **monster-tamer + Metroidvania
  hybrid** (like Monster Sanctuary) merges #1 and #3.

**Honorable mentions:** 2D platformer (*Super Mario Bros.* lane — easiest to build, most
saturated market), and quirky turn-based RPG (*EarthBound* lane — SNES, but music-rich and
writing-driven if storytelling is your strength).

## Storefront Feasibility (where you can actually sell it)

| Store | Realistic? | Cost / Barrier | Notes for a solo dev |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steam** | ✅ **Primary target** | $100 one-time per game (Steam Direct) | Best fit by far. The audience for premium retro-inspired indies (Tunic, Hollow Knight, Axiom Verge) lives here. Easiest to publish, best discoverability, supports paid-upfront pricing. **Start here.** |
| **Nintendo eShop (Switch)** | ✅ Yes, but later | Free to *apply* to the Nintendo Developer Portal; need a legit entity + a real game; dev-kit purchase + approval process | Very doable with your *original* game — Tunic, Axiom Verge, Cassette Beasts all shipped here. Realistically done *after* Steam traction, often via a porting partner. You're selling your original IP, not a Nintendo clone. |
| **Apple App Store** | ⚠️ Possible, not ideal | $99/year Apple Developer | Premium paid games struggle on mobile (race-to-free / IAP economics). Touch controls are awkward for action games. Best as a *later port*. |
| **Google Play** | ⚠️ Possible, not ideal | $25 one-time | Same caveats as Apple, cheaper entry. Same recommendation: port it later. |

**Recommended sequence:** Steam first (prove it, earn the most per sale) → Switch eShop port
(prestige + a hungry audience for this exact genre) → mobile ports last (if controls adapt
cleanly). For "at least one store": **Steam is the answer** — lowest barrier, best-matched
audience, premium pricing.

## Recommended path forward

**Pick #1 (Metroidvania) or the #1+#3 hybrid (monster-tamer Metroidvania) as the first game.**
It maximizes music as a differentiator, is the most achievable solo, has the strongest proven
track record on the best storefront (Steam), and is the cleanest legally. Save the full
*Pokémon*-scale creature-collector for game #2 once you've shipped once.

**Tooling (all free / cheap, all export to Steam + Switch + mobile):**
- **Godot 4** — free, open-source, no royalties, excellent 2D, exports to all target platforms.
  Best fit for a solo dev doing a 2D Metroidvania/Zelda-like. *(Top recommendation.)*
- **Unity** — viable, larger asset store, but licensing history makes Godot the safer default.
- **GameMaker** — strong 2D pedigree, good if you prefer it.
- **Music:** any DAW (Reaper is cheap/excellent) + your guitar/drums → export loopable OGG/WAV
  stems for adaptive in-game music (e.g. a combat layer fades in on boss fights).
