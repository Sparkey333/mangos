# The On-The-Spot Pitch

The goal of a cold, in-person moment is **not to close**. It's to earn the next
small thing: 30 more seconds, a saved contact, a "send that to me." Aim there and
you'll win way more often — and sound relaxed instead of hungry.

This page is a system with three lengths. Fill it once per project. Memorize only
the 10-second line; improvise the rest *from* it.

---

## The three lengths

Think of it like a zoom lens. Same picture, three focal lengths. You read the
other person's eyes and pick one.

### 1. The 10-second line (the "logline")
One breath. No jargon. Said like you've said it a thousand times because you have.

> **Template:** "I'm building **[thing]** that helps **[who]** **[do what]** without
> **[the usual pain]**."

> **Filled example:** "I'm building a tool that helps small contractors send
> professional quotes from their phone in under a minute, instead of fighting
> spreadsheets in the truck."

Rules:
- A 12-year-old should understand it.
- Name the *pain you remove*, not the *tech you use*.
- End slightly up, like a door opening — invites the "oh, how?"

### 2. The 30-second version (the "so-what")
You earned a follow-up question. Now add stakes and traction.

> **Template:**
> "Right now, **[who]** has to **[painful status quo]** — it's **[cost: time/money/
> stress]**. We make it **[the new way]**. The cool part is **[1 proof point: a
> result, a user, a number, a name]**. We're at **[current stage]**."

> **Filled example:**
> "Right now contractors lose jobs because they quote a day late from a clunky
> spreadsheet. We turn a 30-minute quote into a 60-second one, branded, from the
> phone. Early guys are closing more because they're first in. We're piloting with
> about a dozen crews and opening it up this fall."

The "cool part" is the whole game. One concrete proof beats five adjectives.

### 3. The 2-minute version (the "story")
Only if they lean in. This is a story, not a spec sheet. Shape:

1. **The person** — "A buddy of mine, a contractor, lost a $40k job by a day…"
2. **The pattern** — "…and I realized that's not bad luck, it's a tooling problem
   the whole trade has."
3. **The turn** — "So I built the thing I wished he'd had."
4. **Where it's going** — "Now it's [stage], and the dream is [the bigger vision]."
5. **The ask / hand-off** — "Who do you know it'd be perfect for?" or "Can I send
   you the one-pager?"

People forget features. They retell stories. Give them one to retell.

## The hand-off (this is what actually matters)

The pitch's only job is to reach a clean exit. Pre-decide your exits so you're
never fumbling:

- **Default:** "What's the best way to reach you?" → save it *in front of them*,
  text them right there so they have your number too. (A contact saved beats a
  card pocketed, every time.)
- **They're warm:** hand the card/crane (see `business-card-options.md`).
- **They're a real lead:** "I'll send you the one-pager tonight" → then actually
  do it that night (see `leave-behind-one-pager.md`).

> **The golden rule of the close:** make the next step *theirs to receive*, not
> *theirs to do*. "I'll send it" beats "go check out the site."

## Objection / curveball comebacks

Keep these loose and friendly. The tone is "great question," never defensive.

| They say | You say (calm, short) |
|---|---|
| "Isn't this already a thing?" | "Parts of it. What nobody nails is **[your wedge]** — that's the whole point." |
| "How do you make money?" | "**[One line.]** [e.g. 'Flat monthly per crew.'] Simple on purpose." |
| "Aren't you worried someone copies it?" | "Execution and the users we're earning are the moat. Happy to be early." |
| "What makes *you* the one to build it?" | "**[Your unfair advantage — lived it, built it before, obsessed.]**" |
| "Cool… but does it actually work?" | "Yep — **[the proof point again].** Want me to send a 30-sec demo?" |
| (Polite, not interested) | "Totally fair. If **[trigger]** ever comes up, you've got my number." (Exit clean, no chasing.) |

## The practice that makes it "practiced"

- Say the 10-sec line out loud **20 times** today. Out loud, not in your head.
  Smoothness is reps, not talent.
- Record the 30-sec version on your phone once. Listen. Cut every word that isn't
  load-bearing.
- Have **one number and one name** ready for each project. Specifics are the
  difference between "sounds like a dreamer" and "sounds like a founder."
