# One Umbrella or Many? — Red / Blue / Z

> *Strategy, not legal advice. Entity choice has real liability and tax
> consequences that vary by state. Before you file anything, run the final shape
> past a CPA and an attorney in your state. This page gets you to the right
> question fast so that conversation is short.*

The honest headline: **at the idea/pre-revenue stage, the answer is almost always
"one simple thing, set up cheaply, that can grow into anything."** Don't build a
corporate cathedral to house a campfire. But here's the full map so you choose with
eyes open.

---

## 🔴 Option Red — One Bold Umbrella (single entity, many projects under it)

**The shape:** One company (an LLC, often). Every project is a *product line / DBA*
("doing business as") under that one roof. One bank account, one tax return, one
brand of you.

**Symbolism of Red:** unity, a single beating heart, all your fire in one torch.
Red is *commitment to a through-line* — "all my work is one body of work."

**Pros**
- **Cheapest & simplest** — one filing, one registered agent, one annual report,
  one set of books. Minimal cost, minimal admin.
- **One reputation compounds.** Every project builds the *same* brand equity. Great
  if "you" / a studio name is the real asset.
- **Move fast & launch freely** — a new idea is just a new DBA, not a new company.
  Perfect for someone who *stumbles onto ideas* and wants to test them this week.
- **One relationship with the bank, the IRS, the tools.** Less to forget.

**Cons**
- **Liability is shared.** If one project gets sued or buried in debt, *all* the
  assets under that umbrella are exposed — the others included. One bad day can
  splash everything. **This is the big one.**
- **Hard to sell or spin out one project** cleanly later — it's tangled in the
  shared entity. Investors in *one* idea won't love funding *all* of them.
- **Brand blur** if the projects are wildly different (a worship album and a
  contractor SaaS under one name can confuse both audiences).

**Best when:** projects are small, low-liability, early, and thematically related;
and your personal/studio brand is the real through-line. **This is the right
*starting* posture for most people with lots of ideas and little revenue.**

---

## 🔵 Option Blue — Separate Entity Per Project

**The shape:** Each real project gets its own LLC/corp. Walls between them. Each
its own books, brand, bank account, and someday its own investors or buyer.

**Symbolism of Blue:** boundaries, order, deep water between islands, the calm of
knowing a storm on one island won't flood the others. Blue is *protection and
seriousness.*

**Pros**
- **Liability firewalls.** A lawsuit or debt against Project A generally **can't**
  reach Project B's or your personal assets. Each ship has watertight compartments.
- **Clean to fund, partner, or sell** — bring an investor or co-founder into *one*
  project without entangling the rest; sell one without unwinding everything.
- **Distinct brands breathe** — each can have its own identity, audience, voice.
- **Cleaner accounting per project** — you actually know which idea makes money.

**Cons**
- **Cost & admin multiply.** Each entity = its own filing fees, annual reports,
  registered agent, tax return, bank account, bookkeeping. Five ideas = five of
  everything. This **drains time and money fast**, especially before revenue.
- **Overkill for experiments.** Most ideas won't survive contact with the market;
  incorporating each one is building houses for tenants who never move in.
- **Fragmented reputation** — equity doesn't compound across them as cleanly.

**Best when:** a project has *real* revenue, *real* liability exposure, outside
*money/partners*, or a plausible *exit*. **Graduate** an idea into its own Blue
entity when it earns it — not before.

---

## 🟢 Option Z — The Movable Middle (recommended)

**The shape: a Holding Company + spin-outs on demand.** One parent entity is your
"mothership" / brand home (this is your *umbrella you always have set up*). Projects
**start as DBAs under it (cheap, fast — Red energy)**, and any project that proves
itself **graduates into its own subsidiary LLC owned by the holdco (walls go up —
Blue energy)** the moment it has revenue, risk, partners, or a buyer.

Optionally, in states that allow it, a **Series LLC** lets one parent hold multiple
"series," each with its own liability shield, under a single umbrella filing —
literally Red's simplicity with Blue's walls. (Availability and respect-in-court
varies by state; this is exactly the "ask the attorney" spot.)

**Symbolism of Z / the movable middle:** the trellis. One living root system, many
vines, and you decide which vine gets its own trellis as it grows. It's not
indecision — it's *staged commitment*. You don't marry an idea on day one; you date
it as a DBA, and build it a house when it proves it'll stay. Quantum, like you said:
each idea is in superposition — *potential subsidiary* — until reality collapses it
into "graduate this one" or "let this one go," and you craft that call with feel.

**Pros**
- **Start cheap, protect later** — you get Red's speed now and Blue's safety exactly
  when (and only when) a project earns the cost.
- **One umbrella brand to "always have set up,"** which is literally what you asked
  for — the mothership exists from day one and never has to be rebuilt.
- **Clean graduation path** — spinning a DBA into a holdco-owned subsidiary is a
  known, lawyer-friendly move; investors and buyers understand it instantly.
- **Tax & ownership flexibility** — the holdco can own the IP, license it down, and
  consolidate the upside.

**Cons**
- **Slightly more thought up front** — you set up the holdco intentionally and keep
  a clean rule for *when* a project graduates (see trigger list below).
- **Series LLC is state-dependent** and not battle-tested everywhere; don't lean on
  it without local legal confirmation.
- **Discipline required** — the firewalls only hold if you keep the books and bank
  accounts genuinely separate (commingling funds can collapse the protection — ask
  your attorney about "piercing the corporate veil").

**Best when:** you have many ideas, a few might get real, you want one durable brand
home, and you refuse to choose between "fast and fun" and "safe and serious." **This
is the recommendation.**

---

## The graduation trigger (when a DBA becomes its own entity)

Spin a project out of the umbrella into its own subsidiary the moment **any** of
these go true — this is your movable line, made concrete:

- 💵 It's making **meaningful revenue** (real money worth protecting).
- ⚠️ It carries **real liability** (physical products, contracts, client work,
  anything that could get sued).
- 🤝 A **partner or investor** is coming in (they want clean, separate ownership).
- 🚪 There's a plausible **exit** (someone might buy *this one thing*).
- 🧱 It needs a **distinct brand/audience** that the umbrella would muddy.

Until then, keep it a cheap DBA. Let the market, not your filing fees, decide which
ideas are real.

---

## What to actually do first (this week, cheaply)

1. **Form one holding LLC** — your umbrella/mothership. Pick a name broad enough to
   parent anything (your studio name, not a single-project name). This is the
   "always have one set up" piece, done.
2. **Get an EIN** (free, IRS, ~10 min online) and **one business bank account.**
3. **Run new ideas as DBAs** under it — register a DBA only when an idea has a real
   public face. Most ideas won't need even that yet.
4. **Keep money clean** — every dollar in/out through the business account, simple
   bookkeeping from day one. This is what *makes* the future firewalls real.
5. **Book one hour with a CPA/attorney** in your state to confirm: best entity type
   for your tax situation, whether a Series LLC is worth it where you live, and the
   home-state vs. Delaware/Wyoming question (usually: **form in your home state**
   unless you're raising venture money — don't let the internet talk you into
   Delaware prematurely).

> **Bottom line:** Build **one umbrella now (Z)**, run ideas under it as cheap DBAs,
> and graduate the winners into their own walls when they earn it. You get to move
> fast *and* sleep well — which is the whole point.
